# Art direction and image generation

The final scene uses three fixed production images. ImageGen generated the layers separately so the camera could move the Earth, carrier and pilot at different rates. The quoted prompts are the text used for the three selected images. The repository includes those exact PNGs so the render does not depend on recreating a stochastic generation.

## Pilot’s seat

The first input was the pilot’s-eye movie frame. Side views of the linear seat and the model photograph established the physical arrangement. The output is a 1672 × 941 RGBA cel. Black monitor faces leave room for the five flight displays drawn in code. [Production foreground](../artwork/linear-seat.png)

> Draw a new high-resolution animation production foreground cel with a truly transparent alpha background, 16:9 landscape, as wide and detailed as possible. This is a remake of the pilot-eye viewpoint of the RX-0 Unicorn Gundam cockpit from the provided movie reference stills. The FIRST image is the main design and first-person reference. The other images explain the open panoramic linear seat from outside only. We must see from the PILOT'S OWN EYES; there is no pilot face, head or external body visible. Frame a slightly wider, more forward gaze than the down-looking first reference, as if lifting one's eyes to fly: the film-authentic bank of instruments, the hands, and two white-clad knees occupy the LOWER 48% of the final image, while virtually the entire TOP 52% of the cel is PURE TRANSPARENT EMPTY SPACE to composite a separate space panorama. No aircraft canopy, pillars or roof. It is the open seat in a near-invisible spherical panoramic monitor. First-person anatomy: two foreshortened WHITE pressure-suit knees visible in bottom centre, one white-gloved left hand and one white-gloved right hand on separate compact black side grips AT THEIR RESPECTIVE outer arm rests, deep blue cuff and subtle thin red suit seams, remaining sleeves enter from lower corners. Hands stay below the display rims. Display layout is vitally important: in front between the knees on ONE narrow central stalk, a wide straight-on landscape dark blank glass monitor near lower midline, occupying about 30% of total frame width, a slim chamfered grey bezel and four small hardware keys along the bottom. Immediately flanking it are FOUR separate black portrait displays, two on the left and two on the right, each visible and gently angled inward toward pilot like the first reference. Tilted graphite side armrests, light-grey armrest armor shells with narrow green status edge lights, blue-black recessed mechanical details. All FIVE screens are perfectly dark indigo unlit glass with obvious clean corners for later replacement. Lower panel gaps can show transparent space. Aircraft-movie-quality traditional Japanese 2D mechanical cel painting: purposeful hand-inked dark linework, clean sharply defined two-tone and three-tone shadows, restrained cool blue reflected lighting, accurate mechanical design, cool blue-grey shadow planes, flat off-white fabric shading with precise joints. Match the drawing language and proportions of the references, not live-action. Characters' knees, not just arms, must appear. Seat fills bottom and reaches left and right frame edges. Produce an original distinct frame for a composited video, no text overlays or shot from the third-person reference. Absolutely no scenery / stars / colored backdrop in the empty alpha, no title/caption outside the physical objects, no decorative glow haze. Do not include cockpit struts in the empty upper view.

## Formation carrier

The production used the side-view still of the seated pilot with a white ship behind him as the visual guide. The output is a 1672 × 941 RGBA cel. The renderer positions it above Earth and adds the four animated exhaust plumes. [Production carrier](../artwork/formation-carrier.png)

> Use the provided cel from Gundam Unicorn as a design reference for the distant WHITE SPACE CARRIER behind the pilot. Generate an original stand-alone animation production CEL of JUST that white and slate-grey angular Universal Century carrier / landing ship as seen from above and off its stern-quarter. It travels TOWARD THE LEFT, the longer pointed forward deck/bow to the left, raised stern and two to four small blue-violet engine ports at RIGHT. It flies horizontally across a panoramic cockpit display. Long layered white/grey hull with very distinctive blocky flat flight deck, a little bridge tower, charcoal hangar openings, long ventral section, big vertical blade tail, a few small crimson or navy markings. Broad readable shape perspective like the background ship of the reference, realistic engineering scale. No weapons firing. Mechanical 2D Japanese feature anime cel painting, crisp thin dark-navy ink, sharp three-tone steel shadows, sunlit ivory upper plating directionally illuminated from upper LEFT, cool periwinkle blue planet-light underneath, a little blue engine glow at the stern tips. Frame the WHOLE ship with generous margins on ALL sides. Landscape 16:9 transparent PNG production sprite, no sea, no clouds, no planet, no cockpit, no sky, no people, no ground shadow, no text or title, no coloured soft background. All space outside the ship itself must be completely transparent alpha including all openings. Do not generate long exhaust tails; they'll be animated in compositing.

## Earth and atmosphere

This environment was generated from text. It is an 1860 × 845 RGB panorama. An earlier composition called for an orbital station, which explains the open space in the prompt. The final composition puts the carrier there. [Production background](../artwork/earth-orbit.png)

> Use case: stylized-concept. High resolution VFX background environment plate for a cinematic spaceflight, ultrawide landscape 2.2:1, maximum available resolution. View from low Earth orbit with a sweeping curved blue planet filling the lower 43 percent of the shot. A continuous photographic woven carpet of detailed broad cloud systems above a dark blue ocean and faint warm land near the terminator. A luminous paper-thin cyan atmospheric edge, soft restrained amber dawn concentrated towards the far LEFT limb; horizon curves and descends a few degrees toward the RIGHT. Upper half is rich midnight blue to black open space with sparse delicate dim pinprick stars. Natural naturalistic space photography, 65 mm cinema-camera colour, immaculate fine spatial detail, no crushed clouds or clip-white glow. The sun is out of frame to the far left; plenty of spacious open negative space on the right above the limb for a later station composite. The only subject is the planet, atmosphere and faraway stars. No vehicles, no satellites, no station, no frames, no spacecraft parts, no cockpit, no text, no oversaturated nebula, no lens flares, no artificial grid. Edge-to-edge orbital panorama to use as oversized moving environment in a film.

The art direction changed again after the first composite. A blue wireframe mobile suit on the centre monitor looked like a menu. The revised version uses an artificial horizon, velocity and altitude tapes, a heading strip and linked side displays. All of those changes live in the atlas and shader, so the pilot’s hands and physical bezels remain part of the same approved cockpit painting.
